# Opportunity evaluation

Evaluate severity, frequency, demand evidence, willingness to pay, customer accessibility, technical feasibility, AI suitability, MVP effort, capital requirements, alternatives, differentiation, distribution, defensibility, and regulatory/operational complexity.

For each dimension record an evidence-backed assessment, unknowns, and the next question. Use qualitative labels such as favorable, mixed, unfavorable, and unknown with written reasons. These are subjective judgments, not measured confidence. Do not sum scores into an automatic launch or promotion threshold.

Competitors can demonstrate demand; investigate why existing options do or do not meet a specific customer need. Compare non-AI alternatives and operational fixes. Treat revenue, pricing, distribution, and defensibility as hypotheses until supported. Identify disconfirming tests and reasons the founder might be poorly placed to win.

A recommendation should explain the strongest evidence, strongest objection, founder fit, and highest-value next investigation. The founder alone authorizes transition to execution. Draft interview guides, demand tests, MVP requirements, technical proposals, positioning, pricing experiments, and go-to-market hypotheses may follow, but contacting people or running real experiments requires approval.
