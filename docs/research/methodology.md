# Problem-first methodology

Start with customer work and failure modes, not a product pitch. Explore broadly before increasing depth. Focus primarily on US customers, accessible early buyers, low capital needs, and feasible MVPs; traditional industries are in scope.

1. Discover a signal in permitted public sources and search for duplicates.
2. Record the customer, task, friction, context, evidence, and unknowns.
3. Investigate recurrence, consequences, workarounds, alternatives, demand, and willingness to pay.
4. Seek contradictory evidence and explanations that undermine the opportunity.
5. Assess potential solution directions separately from the problem.
6. Recommend the next information-gaining action; do not infer commercial validation from plausibility.

Discovery can leave most questions unknown. A completed investigation must connect meaningful factual claims to source records or label them unsupported hypotheses. Preserve observations separately from interpretations, estimates, and speculation. Capture geographic and temporal limits.

Use forums, reviews, competitor documentation, industry reports, academia, government data, public workflows, and other relevant sources without fixed quotas. Respect access restrictions, rate limits, robots policies where applicable, copyright, privacy, and platform terms. Never bypass authentication or paywalls.

Promote depth when the uncertainty reduction justifies the cost. Stop or pause low-value exploration with a reason. Reopen rejected work only with new evidence or a changed assumption, preserving the earlier rationale.
